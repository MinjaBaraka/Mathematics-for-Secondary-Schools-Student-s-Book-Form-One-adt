Figure 6.3: A horizontal line DE with zero gradient

The gradient of the straight line joining the two points D(x1,y1) and E(x2,y1) can be obtained as follows:

From,
m=y2−y1x2−x1.
But, y2=y1 and x1≠x2. It implies that,
m=y1−y1x2−x1
=0x2−x1
= 0

Therefore, the gradient of a horizontal line DE is 0.

Since the gradient of the line segment DE is zero, then the line DE is horizontal. On the other hand, if the line is vertical, the change in x-values is zero which makes the denominator zero. Consider the line PN in Figure 6.4.
