Example 6.6

The gradient of the line passing through the points P(3, 5) and Q(x, 1) is 43. Find the value of x.

Solution

Given the points P (3, 5) and Q (x, 1). The gradient of the line joining the two points is given by,

m=y2−y1x2−x1

Let, (x1,y1)=(x,1),(x2,y2)=(3,5), and m=43. Substitute these values into the formula for gradient to get,

Thus,

43=5−13−x
43=43−x

Cross multiplication gives,

4(3-x)=12
12-4x=12
-4x=0

Therefore, the value of x is 0.

In Examples 6.3, 6.4, and 6.5, it can be observed that the values of the gradient are non-zero. Thus, only slanted lines have non-zero gradients. Moreover, a line slants towards right (↗) if its gradient is positive as in Example 6.4 and slants towards left (↖) if its gradient is negative as in Example 6.5.

However, horizontal and vertical lines have unique gradients. If the line is horizontal, the change in y-values is zero and the gradient of the line is zero, as shown by the line DE in Figure 6.3.
