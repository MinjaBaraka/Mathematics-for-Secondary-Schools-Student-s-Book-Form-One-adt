Solution

(a) The gradient of the line joining B(1, 2) and C(0, −1) is given by
m=y2−y1x2−x1
Let (x1,y1)=(0,−1) and (x2,y2)=(1,2).
Thus,
m=2−(−1)1−0
=31
=3
Therefore, the gradient of line BC is 3.
(b) The gradient of the line joining B (1, 2) and D (−1, − 4) is given by
m=y2−y1x2−x1
Let (x1,y1)=(1,2) and (x2,y2)=(−1,−4)
Thus,
m=−4−2−1−1
=−6−2
=3
Therefore, the gradient of BD is 3.
In this example, it can be observed that the gradient of a straight line does not change when different pair of points are used provided that all the points lie on the same line.
