Solving linear simultaneous equations by substitution method

Substitution method involves choosing one equation and transposing one of its variables by making it the subject of the other. The resulting equation is substituted into the other to obtain a single equation with one unknown. The following are the steps for solving simultaneous equations of two unknowns by substitution method.

Step 1:Express one of the variables in terms of the other from one of the equations.
Step 2:Substitute the equation obtained in Step 1 into the second equation to obtain an equation in one unknown.
Step 3:Solve the equation in one unknown obtained in Step 2.
Step 4:Substitute the value of the variable obtained in Step 3 to one of the equations to get the value of the other unknown.

Example 5.34

{3y−2x=−7y+2x=11

Solution

Label the two equations as follows.

{3y−2x=−7(1)y+2x=11(2)

From y+2x=11, it implies that,

y=11−2x(3)

Substituting equation (3) into equation (1) gives,

3(11−2x)−2x=−7

Expanding and collecting like terms gives,

−8x=−40
x=408
=5

Substitute x=5 into equation (3), that is,

y=11−(2×5)
=11−10
=1

Therefore, x=5 and y=1.
