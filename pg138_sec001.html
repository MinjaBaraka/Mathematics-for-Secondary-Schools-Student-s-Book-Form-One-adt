Equation (3) is an equation of a straight line passing through points P(x1,y1) and Q(x2,y2) with m as the gradient and c is the value of y at the point where the line crosses y-axis. In this case, y-intercept is point (0,c) since the line will intersect the y-axis if the x-coordinate is 0.

The x-intercept (a point where the line intersects the x-axis) is found when the y-coordinate is 0. Setting y=0 in y=mx+c and solving for x gives,

y = mx + c
0 = mx + c
x=−cm
Thus, the x-intercept is (−cm,0).

In general, if two points (x1,y1) and (x2,y2) lie on a straight line, then any other point (x,y) on a line can be used to obtain the equation of the straight line using the formula,

y−y1x−x1=y2−y1x2−x1
