Gradient of a straight line

A straight line is formed by joining two distinct points. Any two points lying on the straight line can be used to define it. The nature of the straight line is described by its gradient. That is, it can be horizontal, vertical or slanted. The concept of gradient is applicable in many real-life activities such as lifting heavy loads, climbing mountains, determining steepness during road construction and many other applications. A gradient is a measure of steepness of a straight line and it is usually denoted by m. The other name of gradient is slope. In coordinate geometry, the standard way to define the gradient of a straight line is by finding the ratio between the change in y (vertical increase) to the change in x (horizontal increase), that is,
